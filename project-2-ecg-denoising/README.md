# ECG Denoising Pipeline Comparison

Personal practice project comparing four classic denoising approaches
(and a realistic combined clinical pipeline) on the same noisy ECG
signal — where the clean ground truth is known, so SNR improvement and
RMSE are objective scores rather than visual impressions.

This project runs **two separate experiments**, and their numbers are never
mixed:

| Experiment | Data | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic (seeded generator) | generated by `src/generate_noisy_signal.py` | `outputs/metrics.json` |
| **Real PhysioNet NSTDB** | MIT-BIH records + real recorded noise | `outputs/real/` |

## ⚠️ Data sources — please read

### Synthetic pipeline (original build)

Built without internet access to PhysioNet: the ECG is **synthetic**
(`src/generate_noisy_signal.py`), a sum-of-Gaussians clean generator with
three independently-injected artifact sources on top — baseline wander,
50 Hz powerline interference, and EMG-like broadband noise. That clean
reference is exactly what enables the objective scoring.

### Real pipeline (added later)

The same five denoising methods now also run on the **MIT-BIH Noise Stress
Test Database (NSTDB)** from PhysioNet, downloaded into
`data/physionet/nstdb/` plus `data/physionet/mitdb/`:

- **Clean ECG** comes from real MIT-BIH records (default `100`, `101`),
  so the "clean" reference is a real recording rather than a generator.
- **Noise** comes from the three real recorded noise records — `bw`
  (baseline wander), `ma` (muscle/EMG artifact), `em` (electrode motion
  artifact) — recorded from physically active volunteers with standard
  ECG equipment.
- The two are mixed at a **precisely controlled target SNR** by
  `src/noise_mixer.py`, which resamples to a common rate, aligns, and
  scales the noise so the achieved SNR equals the requested SNR to within
  1e-9 dB (verified by test).

**Downloaded PhysioNet data is never committed to this repository** —
`data/` is git-ignored. Re-download with:

```bash
python -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0, 'src'); from load_real_ecg import download_records; download_records()"
python -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0, 'src'); from load_real_noise import ensure_downloaded; ensure_downloaded()"
```

**This is a learning/demonstration project**, not a clinical tool, and no
clinical claim is made or implied.

## What it does

1. **Corruption** (`generate_noisy_signal.py`) — 30 s of clean synthetic
   ECG at 250 Hz, corrupted with baseline wander, 50 Hz powerline
   pickup, and EMG-like broadband noise.
2. **Real mixing** (`load_real_ecg.py`, `load_real_noise.py`,
   `noise_mixer.py`) — load real ECG and real noise from PhysioNet and
   mix them at an exact target SNR.
3. **Denoising methods** (`denoise_methods.py`):
   1. Median-filter baseline removal (classic clinical baseline-wander
      correction)
   2. Butterworth bandpass filtering (standard 0.5–40 Hz ECG passband)
   3. IIR notch filter targeted at the powerline frequency
   4. FFT-domain soft-thresholding denoiser (a frequency-domain analogue
      of wavelet soft-thresholding, without needing a wavelet library)
   5. A combined pipeline (1 → 3 → 2), the shape of a real clinical
      front-end chain
4. **Evaluation** — SNR improvement, RMSE, NRMSE, PRD, Pearson
   correlation, and R-peak detector sensitivity/precision/MAE against the
   clean reference, plus plots.

### Scoring convention (DC-offset correction)

Waveform-error metrics (`rmse`, `nrmse`, `prd`, `snr_db`) are computed
after **removing the mean from both** the reference and the estimate.
Real MIT-BIH segments carry a mean of roughly −0.33 mV, and a filter that
legitimately removes DC would otherwise be penalised about 23 dB for doing
its job. This is applied consistently to every method, the synthetic
comparison, and the real experiment, so no method gains an unfair
advantage. `pearson` correlation is unaffected by a constant offset.

## Results

All numbers below are **measured by these runs**; `outputs/metrics.json`
and `outputs/real/results_summary.csv` are the authoritative records.

### Synthetic (seed 3, 30 s at 250 Hz, raw SNR −0.85 dB)

| Method | SNR improvement | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| Butterworth bandpass (0.5–40 Hz) | **+16.34 dB** | 0.0324 |
| Combined pipeline (median → notch → bandpass) | +15.07 dB | 0.0375 |
| FFT soft-thresholding | +8.76 dB | 0.0777 |
| Median-filter baseline removal | +8.63 dB | 0.0788 |
| 50 Hz notch alone | +0.33 dB | 0.2049 |
| *(raw noisy, reference)* | *0.00 dB* | *0.2129* |

Straightforward reading: the bandpass gives the biggest single-step
improvement because it removes all three artifact classes at once; the
combined clinical-style pipeline is close behind while retaining each
step's specific role; the notch alone is nearly neutral — expected, since
powerline content is a small fraction of the total corruption on its own.

> **Note:** an earlier revision of this README reported "+7.17 dB" for the
> bandpass. That figure was polluted by the DC-offset issue described
> above: the raw score included a mean offset that the high-pass stage
> correctly removed. The +16.34 dB figure is the DC-corrected score and is
> the one now produced by `outputs/metrics.json`.

### Real PhysioNet NSTDB (`outputs/real/`)

**Data mixing:** clean MIT-BIH ECG was mixed with the real NSTDB
noise records at an **exactly requested target SNR**.  The mixer
re-scales the noise to hit the target to within ~1e-15 dB: the
experiment prints `Mixer accuracy: max |achieved - target| = 3.55e-15 dB`
and the per-cell `achieved_snr_db` column is verified by
`tests/test_real_experiment.py::test_real_achieved_snr_matches_target`
(asserts deviation < 1e-6 dB).

**Reproducibility — 5 segments per cell.**  Every cell (3 noise types ×
6 SNR levels) was evaluated on **5 independent noise segments per ECG
record** (segment IDs 0–4) with per-segment reproducible seeds
(`np.random.default_rng([seed, seg, target+100, name lengths])`), so the
aggregates are means over 5 windows per cell, not one noisy sample.
Run: `run_real_experiment.py --download --segments 5 --seed 3`.
Result: **1,080 segment rows** (5 × 3 noise × 6 SNR × 2 ECG records × 6
methods); all 6 SNR levels and all 3 noise types present (asserted by
`test_real_segments_have_at_least_five_per_cell` and
`test_real_segments_cover_all_snr_levels_and_noise_types`); 108 summary
rows asserted by `test_real_summary_has_a_row_per_cell_and_method`.

Mean SNR improvement (dB) per cell, averaged over the 5 segments and
both records:

| Target SNR | Bandpass 0.5–40 | Combined | Median | FFT soft-thr. | Notch 50 Hz |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| −6 dB | **+8.22** | +9.67 | +8.65 | +9.14 | +0.01 | 0.00 |
| 0 dB | **+6.04** | +6.78 | +5.21 | +6.66 | +0.00 | 0.00 |
| +6 dB | **+3.76** | +3.65 | +1.73 | +3.94 | +0.00 | 0.00 |
| +12 dB | **−0.31** | −0.73 | −3.19 | −0.26 | −0.01 | 0.00 |
| +18 dB | −5.13 | −5.89 | −8.76 | −5.24 | −0.04 | 0.00 |
| +24 dB | −10.77 | −11.70 | −14.71 | −10.96 | −0.16 | 0.00 |

These are means over 5 segments per cell (2 records × 3 noise types).

What this shows, including the uncomfortable part:

- **Gains are positive and largest at low SNR** (−6 … +6 dB), where there
  is real noise to remove — the physically expected behaviour.
- **Gains become negative at high SNR** (+12, +18, +24 dB). When the
  recording is already clean, filtering removes signal content along with
  almost no noise, so it costs more than it gains. Reporting only the
  low-SNR rows would be cherry-picking; the losses are shown deliberately.
- **The notch alone is essentially neutral across the whole range**
  (−0.16 … +0.01 dB), because powerline is only one of three noise
  sources here.
- **NRMSE falls monotonically with SNR** for the bandpass
  (0.177 → 0.108 → 0.068 → 0.056 → 0.050 → 0.049), so the error metric
  agrees with the SNR metric about ordering the operating points.
- By noise category, the bandpass averages **+15.61 dB on baseline
  wander, +2.96 dB on electrode motion, and +6.08 dB on muscle
  artifact** — electrode motion is the hardest class, consistent with
  the NSTDB documentation noting it "can mimic the appearance of ectopic
  beats and cannot be removed easily by simple filters."
